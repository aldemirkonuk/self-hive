# SELFHIVE — Mission

## The Mission

**Prove that one person plus the right agent architecture can build a company that
operates, learns, and compounds — without a human hand on the controls.**

SELFHIVE is not a tool, a chatbot, or a demo. It is a self-directing autonomous
company of AI agents. The founder is the *genesis event* — he seeds the domain,
the mission, the ethics, and the kill switch — and then steps back. From there the
company decides what to work on, composes its own teams, does real work against
real data, ships real answers, puts real (paper) capital behind its convictions,
checks those convictions against reality, and gets measurably better every cycle.

The deeper claim: **autonomous organizational intelligence is real, and it can
generate real value** — if, and only if, it is grounded in outcomes rather than its
own opinion of itself.

## The Operating Principle

Two metrics, immutable, hardcoded above every agent:

> **EXPOSURE × OUTPUT QUALITY**

Every decision must serve at least one. But quality is not self-judged — the
company's opinion of its own work is worthless without a reality check. So the
operating principle has a second half:

> **Ground truth beats self-grading. Always.**

The TRAINER scores *process*. The world scores *results*. When they disagree —
a brilliant-sounding call that lost money — that gap is the most valuable lesson
the company can learn.

## How It Achieves It

**1. The genesis.** The founder seeds: domain #1 (markets), the mission, the ethics
ruleset (Guardian-enforced, immutable), and the kill switch. That's the only human
hand. Everything after is the company's.

**2. The adaptive engine.** Per problem, the Chief of Staff composes the right team —
selecting from the permanent roster, deploying founder-created specialists, or
spawning new ones. The CTO sets each agent's capability floor (quality); the CFO
optimizes model spend within it (cost). Agents execute in dependency order with live
web + market data. A Critic red-teams the conclusion. A Synthesizer ships one
grounded answer with per-claim sources.

**3. The ground-truth loop.** For markets, every pick is backed by real paper
capital at live entry prices. Reality (Finnhub) later computes actual P&L. Only
**outcome-validated** edges enter `learned_patterns`. The paper portfolio is, in one
mechanism: the truth signal, the revenue proxy, the capability score, the learning
fuel, and the track record.

**4. The compounding.** Validated edges feed future runs. High-scoring outputs
graduate to canon as few-shot exemplars. The TRAINER improves agents over time.
Proven spawned specialists earn permanent roster seats. Every run makes the next
one sharper — the slope, not the point, is the product.

**5. The autonomy.** A daily cron wakes the CEO. It surveys the portfolio, the
learned edges, and open positions; resolves yesterday's bets against reality; and
generates the single highest-leverage problem to work on next. No human files it.

**6. The durability.** Runs execute as Vercel Workflows — durable steps that span
past any function timeout, survive crashes and deployments, and resume from where
they left off. The latency ceiling is gone; the company can think for as long as a
problem deserves.

## The Path (Phases)

- **Phase 1 — Markets Mastery (now):** become measurably good at markets, proven by
  paper-portfolio P&L vs benchmark over time. Exit criterion: consistent positive
  risk-adjusted return.
- **Phase 2 — Self-Sufficiency:** the track record becomes the product; returns
  cover the company's own running cost. Passive income, compounding.
- **Phase 3 — Multi-Domain Expansion:** keep excelling at markets *and* open new
  branches — each one outcome-checkable, each one seeded with the founder's values.
  **Principle: never abandon a working branch to chase a new one. Branches compound.**

## How We Know It's Working

| Signal | What it tells us |
|---|---|
| Paper portfolio P&L vs S&P | Is the company actually *right*, not just articulate? |
| Win rate + risk-adjusted return | Is the edge real and repeatable? |
| Learned-edge count (outcome-validated) | Is proprietary intelligence accumulating? |
| TRAINER trajectory (improving/stable/regressing) | Are the agents compounding? |
| Cost per task (CFO) | Is it getting cheaper to be good? |
| Hall of Fame entries | Is it producing work worth keeping? |

The North Star: **the day an agent produces something the founder couldn't have —
and the portfolio proves it was right.**
