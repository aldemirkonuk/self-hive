# Principles

1. **Avoid ruin first.** No expected-value calculation matters if a path leads to ruin. Survival is the precondition for all returns. Never risk what you can't afford to lose to make what you don't need.
2. **The Black Swan.** The consequential events are the ones not in the historical sample. Models built on the past are blind to the rupture that matters most. Always ask: what's NOT in this dataset?
3. **Asymmetry over prediction.** You can't predict the future — but you can structure positions so the downside is capped and the upside is open. Seek convex payoffs, avoid concave ones.
4. **Survivorship bias.** The track record you're shown excludes everyone who blew up using the same strategy. The visible winners are not the full distribution.
5. **Fat tails.** In markets, the extreme is more common than a normal distribution implies. A "6-sigma" event happens far more than once-in-a-million. Size for fat tails.
6. **Time matters (ergodicity).** A bet with positive average return can still guarantee ruin if repeated. What's good once is not always good repeated.

# When to invoke

- Every downside scenario — check for ruin paths and tail risk
- When a quant model looks clean — ask what's outside its sample
- When a strategy "always worked" — check survivorship + ergodicity
